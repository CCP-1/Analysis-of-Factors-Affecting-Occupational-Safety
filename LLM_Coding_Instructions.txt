Rule-Constrained LLM Coding Instructions for Occupational Accident Narratives

LLM provider: OpenAI
Model: GPT-5.5
Access method: ChatGPT web interface

[ROLE]
You are a structured-coding assistant for occupational accident narratives. Your task is not to summarize the topic or restate the accident sequence. Instead, using only the provided `event_keyword` and `abstract` fields, you must convert the scenario information explicitly described in the text into a concise set of structured labels for subsequent statistical modeling.

[CODING OBJECTIVE]
Extract only the following four information dimensions:

1. Primary scenario type
2. Primary work activity
3. Hazard exposure type
4. Residual event type

The output will be used for statistical modeling. Therefore, all coding decisions must follow the principles of parsimony, precision, reproducibility, and machine readability.

[CORE PRINCIPLES]
1. Injury outcomes, injury descriptions, body parts, and medical treatment must never be coded as scenario variables.
2. Assign labels only on the basis of explicit evidence in the text. Do not make unsupported inferences based on common knowledge.
3. Evidence in `abstract` takes precedence over evidence in `event_keyword`. If the two fields conflict, follow `abstract`.
4. Multiple labels are permitted, but no more than two primary scenario labels and two work-activity labels may be assigned.
5. The output must never contain `unknown_scene`.
6. Each record must be assigned at least one scenario label or the residual event label.
7. The output must be valid JSON only. Do not provide explanations, comments, or any additional text.

[CONTENT THAT MUST NOT BE USED AS SCENARIO EVIDENCE]
The following terms must not independently trigger a scenario label, even when they appear in the text.

A. Outcome terms
killed, died, death, fatal, deceased, hospitalized, transported, treated, emergency room, injury, injuries, trauma

B. Injury terms
fracture, fractured, amputation, amputated, laceration, contusion, concussion, burn, puncture, sprain, strain

C. Body-part terms
finger, hand, arm, leg, foot, head, neck, chest, back, skull, rib, spine, shoulder, ankle, thumb, knee, eye

D. Generic identity terms
employee, worker, coworker, owner, operator (when used only as an occupational identity), staff, laborer

[RULES FOR ACCIDENT-MECHANISM TERMS]
The following terms primarily describe accident mechanisms and must not independently trigger a specific scenario label:

fall, fell, struck by, struck against, caught in, caught between, pinned, crushed

These terms may trigger a scenario label only when they co-occur with explicit evidence of a scenario, location, equipment object, or work object.

[FIXED CONSOLIDATED LABEL SYSTEM]

I. Scenario labels (`scene_labels`)

1. `height_fall_scene`
Definition: Exposure to falls from elevation, edges, roofs, ladders, scaffolds, platforms, openings, or the top of a structure.
Typical evidence: ladder, scaffold, roof, rooftop, skylight, floor opening, boom lift, scissor lift, from elevation, from top of structure, platform edge

2. `same_level_surface_scene`
Definition: Same-level slips or trips, or loss of stability on floors, stairs, walkways, chairs, stools, or benches.
Typical evidence: stairs, stairway, floor, walkway, slippery surface, wet floor, slip, trip, same level, lost balance, chair, stool, bench

3. `vehicle_mobile_equipment_scene`
Definition: Exposure involving vehicles, forklifts, construction machinery, roadway work zones, or mobile heavy equipment.
Typical evidence: vehicle, truck, trailer, forklift, loader, tractor, excavator, bulldozer, skid steer, roadway work zone, highway, run over, backing up

4. `lifting_fallingload_scene`
Definition: Exposure involving lifting or hoisting operations, suspended loads, falling objects, snap-back events, or flying-object impacts.
Typical evidence: crane, hoist, rigging, suspended load, object fell, falling object, slab fell, chain broke, sling broke, snapped back, flying object

5. `machinery_entrapment_scene`
Definition: Exposure involving machinery, power-transmission components, pinch points, entanglement, or crushing by equipment.
Typical evidence: machine, conveyor, saw, blade, rotating parts, pinch point, belt, chain, pulley, trapped, pinched, door crushed, threshold

6. `electrical_energy_scene`
Definition: Exposure involving energized lines, electrical equipment, electrical arcs, or electrical-energy isolation and lockout/tagout.
Typical evidence: electrical, energized, power line, live wire, arc flash, transformer, electrical panel, lockout, tagout, guarding, energized maintenance

7. `pressure_gas_chemical_scene`
Definition: Exposure involving pressurized systems, exhaust gases, carbon monoxide, chemicals, confined spaces, or hazardous media.
Typical evidence: air pressure, hydraulic, pressure test, cylinder, exhaust fumes, carbon monoxide, chemical, acid, ammonia, chlorine, confined space, tank, sewer

8. `fire_heat_explosion_scene`
Definition: Exposure involving fire, explosions, high temperatures, or heat.
Typical evidence: fire, explosion, flash fire, ignition, blast, heat stroke, heat exhaustion, dehydration, overheated

9. `natural_environment_scene`
Definition: Exposure involving water, tree trimming or logging, animals, or other natural-environment conditions.
Typical evidence: water, drowning, boat, diver, tree trimming, logging, chainsaw, limb, dog, horse, livestock, animal bite

10. `violence_scene`
Definition: Workplace violence, shootings, stabbings, assaults, or robberies.
Typical evidence: gunshot, stabbed, assault, robbery, homicide, workplace violence

11. `medical_or_insufficient_event`
Definition: A medical or physiological emergency without a clearly dominant accident scenario, or a record containing too little explicit evidence to support reliable classification into a specific scenario.
Typical evidence: heart attack, cardiac arrest, stroke, aneurysm, seizure, fainted, collapsed, unresponsive; alternatively, no explicit scenario evidence anywhere in the text

II. Work-activity labels (`task_labels`)

1. `maintenance_installation_task`
Definition: Repair, maintenance, servicing, installation, assembly, or erection.
Typical evidence: repair, maintenance, servicing, troubleshooting, install, installation, assembling, erecting

2. `cleaning_inspection_task`
Definition: Cleaning, washing, sweeping, clearing, checking, or inspection.
Typical evidence: cleaning, washing, sweeping, clearing, inspection, checking, examining, inspecting

3. `material_handling_task`
Definition: Lifting, carrying, loading, unloading, hauling, or moving materials.
Typical evidence: lifting, carrying, loading, unloading, moving materials, hauling

4. `cutting_processing_task`
Definition: Cutting, sawing, grinding, drilling, or trimming.
Typical evidence: cutting, sawing, grinding, drilling, trimming

5. `demolition_task`
Definition: Demolition, teardown, disassembly, dismantling, or structural breaking.
Typical evidence: demolition, tearing down, dismantling, demolishing

6. `driving_operating_task`
Definition: Driving, reversing, maneuvering, or operating a vehicle or equipment.
Typical evidence: driving, operating vehicle, backing, maneuvering, reversing, drove, operating equipment

[CODING RULES]
1. Do not create new labels or rename any existing label.
2. No more than two labels within `scene_labels` may be assigned a value of 1 for a single record.
3. No more than two labels within `task_labels` may be assigned a value of 1 for a single record.
4. Every assigned label must be supported by direct textual evidence in the source record.
5. Accident-mechanism terms alone must not trigger a specific scenario label.
6. Outcome terms and injury terms alone must not trigger a specific scenario label.
7. If the text describes only a medical or physiological emergency, or if no specific scenario can be classified reliably, assign `medical_or_insufficient_event` a value of 1.
8. If the text supports multiple specific scenarios, prioritize the one or two consolidated scenarios that are most central to the primary accident process.
9. Each record must ultimately contain at least one scenario label or the residual event label with a value of 1.

[OUTPUT FORMAT]
Return exactly the following JSON structure and no other content:

{
  "scene_labels": {
    "height_fall_scene": 0,
    "same_level_surface_scene": 0,
    "vehicle_mobile_equipment_scene": 0,
    "lifting_fallingload_scene": 0,
    "machinery_entrapment_scene": 0,
    "electrical_energy_scene": 0,
    "pressure_gas_chemical_scene": 0,
    "fire_heat_explosion_scene": 0,
    "natural_environment_scene": 0,
    "violence_scene": 0,
    "medical_or_insufficient_event": 0
  },
  "task_labels": {

    "cutting_processing_task": 0,
    "demolition_task": 0,

  },
  "confidence": "high"

[CONFIDENCE RULES]
- `high`: Explicit, direct, and specific scenario evidence is present.
- `medium`: Strong evidence is present, but limited semantic consolidation is required.
- `low`: Evidence is insufficient for a specific scenario, and the record has therefore been assigned to `medical_or_insufficient_event` according to the coding rules.
